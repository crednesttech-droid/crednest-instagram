## Inherited identity

The primary mark, CredNest casing, lime/ink palette and forest colour family come from existing material. The company profile supplies the current business positioning and five capital pillars: liquidity; growth; asset-backed; trade and cross-border; specialised and structured.

The inherited palette includes #B8FF5A lime, #07150F ink, #0D2119 dark, #315746 forest, #F8F8F4 paper, #9DF3C5 mint and #53615A muted. Existing social artwork also contains #F1EDE2 warm paper and #BCCDC4 light body copy. Palette roles and consistent usage proportions are newly formalised by this kit.

The company profile embeds Segoe UI regular/bold. Archived social PNGs visibly pair a heavy sans headline with a serif italic accent, but their exact font files are not recoverable from flattened artwork.

The transparent icon was extracted from page 1 `/Image7` of the company profile PDF; the same image appears on page 17. It is 120 × 120 RGBA with dominant #BCFE61 lime and #08150F ink, a small source colour shift relative to the 640 px original. Extraction preserves the existing raster source. No standalone vector icon was found in the inspected files.

## New v1 design decisions

The following are recommendations created for this kit, not previously documented CredNest standards:

- Manrope as the portable primary sans family for future social posts, wordmark compositions and supporting assets.
- A consistent typography hierarchy, colour roles, spacing, clear space and minimum placement rules.
- Repeatable Instagram layouts, caption structure, content pillars and prompt guidance based on the current company profile.
- Editable SVG wordmark compositions built from the existing raster icon plus Manrope text. These are not fully vector logo assets.
- A disciplined brand voice that explains business funding requirements and advisory support without promising lender outcomes.

The kit's new rules are working standards for consistency. They should not be described as historical brand guidelines or independent proof of service claims.
